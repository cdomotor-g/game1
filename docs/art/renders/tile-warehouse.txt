A woodcut illustration from a worn 1600s surveyor's field book, drawn in three-quarter view from high above and to the left, so that the long side wall and the near gable end are both visible at once and the eye looks down onto the roof.

A big plain storehouse of dark tarred boards. Its floor is carried on a row of short round stone pillars with flat wide caps, lifting the whole building a clear foot above the grass so that daylight shows in the gap underneath. A wide double door in the near gable carries a heavy padlock, and above the door a hoist beam projects out from the gable peak with a pulley block hanging from it. On the bare earth apron below the door stand three wooden crates, one bound with rope, and two barrels. Rough grass runs out to every edge of the picture.

Heavy uneven black woodcut outlines; all shading built from hand-drawn hatching and cross-hatching; over-inked pooling where lines meet; bare paper for the lit surfaces. Flat muted spot colour printed slightly out of register beneath the black line, in solid areas with no blending: cold slate blue on the tarred boards and the stone, warm ochre on the crates and barrels, dusty grey-green on the grass. Warm oatmeal paper, warm near-black ink. Plain empty grass fills the lower left corner.
