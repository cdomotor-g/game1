A coloured woodcut illustration of a single long low military barrack range, set at a sharp angle and drawn in three-quarter view from high above and to the left, so that the long front wall and one short end wall are both visible at once and the eye looks down onto the roof.

The range is built of plain dressed stone, its length running directly back into the picture and away towards the far corner, its near end large and its far end small with distance. Its walls are cool pale grey-blue stone blocks, and a shallow hipped roof of grey slate slopes gently down on all four sides to a wide overhanging eave, with one plain chimney at each end. The building is severe, institutional and entirely plain. Along the whole front wall the same small square window is repeated over and over in one unbroken evenly spaced row, six of them on the upper floor and six directly beneath them, every one plain and square and exactly like its neighbours, each big enough to read as its own opening; that relentless repetition is the most obvious thing about the building. A single plain doorway breaks the lower row at its middle. In front of the range a low drystone yard wall crosses the dry bare earth, broken by a square gate of squared timber between two stone piers. In the yard a rack of spears stands upright in a timber frame, and a stout drill post is set in the ground with a worn ring trodden round it.

Composition: a band of plain empty grass runs down the whole left quarter of the page and another down the whole right quarter, each one from the top edge to the bottom edge, with nothing whatever standing in either. Plain empty ground fills the top sixth of the page and the bottom third in the same way, so that the range and its yard together occupy only the middle of the picture with open ground on all four sides of them. The ground above the range is more of that same dry grass, seen from above and running back to the top edge of the page, so that grass rather than anything else is what lies above the roof ridge. Plain dry grass fills all four corners, and the whole of the lower left is bare trodden earth and grass with nothing standing on it.

Strong flat muted spot colour is printed under the black line like a mis-registered letterpress run, in solid areas with no blending and no shading: clearly cool slate blue-grey on the stone walls and on the slate roof, dull weathered ochre on the gate timber and on the spear shafts and rack, dusty grey-green across the grass, dry pale dun on the trodden earth of the yard. Every part of the picture carries one of these flat colours, and all of them are muted and chalky and greyed. Over that colour, heavy uneven black woodcut outlines, with all shading built from hand-drawn hatching and cross-hatching, over-inked pooling where lines meet at sharp angles, and bare paper for the lit surfaces. Warm oatmeal paper, warm near-black ink. Built by hand out of local stone and kept in repair, never new and never ruined.
