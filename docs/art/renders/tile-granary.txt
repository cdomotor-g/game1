A coloured woodcut illustration of a single small farm building, drawn in three-quarter view from high above and to the left, so that the long weatherboarded side wall and the near gable end are both visible at once and the eye looks down onto the roof.

A small grain store standing well clear of the ground. Its floor is carried on four short round stone pillars with flat wide caps, lifting the whole building two feet above the grass so that an open band of daylight runs right underneath the floorboards and the grass is visible through that gap; the open gap under the floor is the most obvious thing about the building. Overlapping weatherboards clad the sides and a steep roof of split shingles comes down over them, with one small louvred vent set high in the gable. There is no door at floor level and no porch: the only way in is a small square hatch door set high up in the near gable, just below the vent, and a short ladder of five rungs leans up against the boards and reaches exactly to the sill of that high hatch. Two plump grain sacks and a wooden scoop stand on the worn earth at the foot of the ladder. Rough grass runs out to every edge of the picture.

Composition: the shingled roof and the boarded body of the store sit across the middle of the page. Everything below the building - the four stone pillars, the open gap, the ladder foot, the two sacks and the scoop - is gathered tightly into the central third of the width, directly beneath the roof. Plain rough grass fills the whole lower left and the whole lower right of the page, and plain rough grass fills all four corners. The roof ridge sits comfortably below the top edge.

Strong flat muted spot colour is printed under the black line like a mis-registered letterpress run, in solid areas with no blending and no shading: clearly warm ochre-brown on the weatherboards and on the shingled roof, clearly cold slate blue-grey on the four stone pillars, clearly dusty grey-green across all the grass, warm ochre on the sacks and the scoop. Every part of the picture carries one of these flat colours. Over that colour, heavy uneven black woodcut outlines, with all shading built from hand-drawn hatching and cross-hatching, over-inked pooling where lines meet at sharp angles, and bare paper for the lit surfaces. Warm oatmeal paper, warm near-black ink. Built by hand out of local timber and kept in repair, never new and never ruined.
