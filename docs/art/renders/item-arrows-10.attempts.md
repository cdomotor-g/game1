## The two departures

**Bound twice, not once.** The brief asks for one binding *about a third of the
way up from the heads*, with the cord ends left long and lying loose across the
shafts. The plate has two bindings, each with its own knot and loose ends. The card's own story says the same as the brief -
*a sheaf of ten, bound at the neck with a twist of cord* - so the singular is the
card's too, not just the brief's. It is the same idea done twice and nothing is
lost by it; a real sheaf is as often bound twice as once, and no rule anywhere
counts bindings.

**No loose feathers and no curl of shaving.** The brief puts two loose feathers
and a shaving on the bench beside the sheaf, as the workshop detail that says the
arrows were made rather than bought. The bench beside the sheaf is bare.

Neither is a reason to redraw. Both are written down because the brief is what the
next attempt would be handed, and somebody comparing the two should not have to
work out which differences were noticed and which were not.

## The aiming caught something worth keeping

The first subject box put the ink's right edge at 89% and the card crop cut the
nocks off the corner. A thin nock is invisible to a percentage-of-scanline
threshold and to a 40-cell `plate-map` alike; the true ink runs to 97%. The
framing note records it. `aim-preview` is what found it, which is what it is for.
