Print at 100% — no “fit to page”, no scaling, on 210 × 297 mm paper. Each cell then comes off the sheet at 16.7 mm across the flats, which is a world-map hex on the korvane-reach map at its four-sheet preset and therefore a mini-map cell: a piece cut out seats where it is meant to, and a figure based for the campaign board stands beside it. A tile printed 4% small is a tile that does not seat, which is why this page offers no paper but the one the pieces are drawn for. For a file rather than paper, print and choose Save as PDF — the page size is already set, so the PDF is true size too.

Two-sided printing. A tile is a piece with two sides: the face is the building finished, the back is that same picture with the colour run not laid on and its name band drawn hollow. Choose both sides, two-sided printing and every other sheet is a run of backs laid out mirrored, so each one falls under its own face when the paper is turned — which means the flip in your printer's dialog has to be the flip chosen above. Get it the wrong way round and every back lands on the wrong tile. No duplex printer: choose side by side, cut both, and glue them together.

Pieces are laid out with their 2 mm bleed showing and a 2 mm gutter between them. The cut line drawn inside each one is the hexagon to follow; the bleed is the margin for cutting it crookedly.

Three or four of the cheap tier-1 tiles per player, one or two of everything above tier 2, and one of anything marked unique. Buildings are built more than once and by more than one player, so a tile is a supply piece rather than a card.
