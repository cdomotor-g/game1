# Discovery and encounters

The land does something back. Every time a figure, a party or a vehicle ends a leg, a d20 on the table for the ground it stopped on says what: nothing, most often; a merchant, a traveller, bandits, a trace worth surveying, a cave, a monster, or an omen. This chapter is the roll, every result, and how a living thing you meet is run.

## The roll

1. When a leg ends — a day leg or a night leg — roll a {{discovery.die}} on the discovery table for the hex you stopped in. One roll for the leg, never one per hex crossed.
2. Read the table by the hex's terrain letter. A hex with a road or a rail line on it uses the road or rail table instead of its ground.
3. Do what the result says. A result that persists — a monster, a trace, a cave, a quest site — is marked on the hex with a tile or a figure so the whole table can see the world filling in.

The tables are in Annex I, *Discovery tables*. Roads grow merchants, travellers and bandits; desert, mountain and marsh grow monsters; a trace is rare everywhere, because discovery is what you find when you are not looking. Surveying, foraging and hunting are jobs of their own, and a discovery roll never stands in for one.

### Moving the bands

Each table's monster band can be widened or shrunk. A band widens by claiming that many rolls below its printed bottom; it never claims 1 and never claims 20, which is always the omen. A band shrunk past its own top simply does not occur. The modifiers stack.

| When | The monster band |
| --- | --- |
| The roll after a night leg | widens by 1 |
| Within one hex of any settlement | shrinks by 2 |
| While Blood Moon is in play | widens by 3 |
| While the Quiet Season is in play | shrinks by 2 |

A party with binoculars may re-roll one discovery roll a round and keep the second. A party with a lit lantern under a Blood Moon may always withdraw before a monster encounter begins.

## The results

**Nothing.** {{discovery.results[id=nothing].summary}}

**Travelling merchant.** Shuffle the items deck and deal {{rules.market.merchantStock.roadside}} cards face up; that is his stock, at base value plus a tenth. He has also heard prices: learn the current price of one commodity family in the nearest settlement.

**Traveller.** News on the road. Look at the top card of the event deck and put it back, or look at the top card of the quest deck.

**Bandits.** Strength 2, plus 1 for every full 20 bulk of cargo the party carries. Fight them, or pay a toll of a quarter of the value of what you are carrying. An escort — a soldier or a hireling — rolls first.

**Monster.** Draw from the monster deck until the drawn monster's ground includes this hex's terrain, shuffling the others back. Deal it onto the encounter board and resolve it — [Monsters and battle](12-battle.md).

**Trace.** Something worth digging showed in a cutting or a stream bed. Put a trace tile on the hex: a survey here is at +2 until the deposit is found or ruled out.

**Cave mouth.** Put a cave tile on the hex. A party with a lit torch or lantern may enter, now or on any later visit; entering resolves one draw on the cave table — hazard, nothing, a trace, a monster, a hoard or an omen. A cave once emptied is marked spent and becomes a known camp ([The map and travel](08-the-map-and-travel.md)).

**Omen.** Something stranger than the road. Draw the top card of the quest deck; accept it or put it on the bottom ([Quests and campaigns](15-quests-and-campaigns.md)).

**Hazard.** A washed-out track, a sand-slip, rotten ice. The leg ends here, and each character takes 1 damage unless the party discards a coil of rope or lets one tool take 4 wear.

**Pirates.** Strength 3. Fight, pay three tenths of the cargo's value, or run and lose half your pace on your next leg.

**Flotsam.** A wreck, or the leavings of one. Gain 2 bulk of one commodity, chosen at random from the *Commodities* table in Annex I, and rolled again if it is livestock.

**Hoard.** Something laired here once and left in a hurry. Gain 20 coin and one card dealt from the items deck.

> **Open.** Bandits and pirates print a strength and nothing else. Until they are given a card: they fight with armour 0, one exchange settles it, and a party that loses the exchange pays the toll as well as the wound.

## Running an encounter

A discovery roll that turns up a living thing hands it to the **encounter board**, the spare player board every table prints ([On the table](02-on-the-table.md)):

1. Shuffle the deck the result names — monsters for a beast, characters for a stranger on the road — and deal one card face up into the board's IN PLAY recess.
2. Set the board's tracks from the card's strip, exactly as a player sets their own: H, S, P and M. Armour is printed and does not move.
3. The player to the left of whoever drew it runs it, as a player who is not a person. It now has everything a player has.
4. When the encounter ends, put the card away: back into its deck; onto the hex as a lair, if the monster is left unresolved; or into the winner's hand, if it was befriended, enslaved or domesticated. Clear the board. Nothing is written down.

A monster left on its hex is a **lair**. It attacks any party that ends a leg there, and event cards may move it. The wild accumulates.

A stranger dealt from the character deck is a character like any other, run by the player to the left for the length of the encounter. What they want of you is theirs to say.
